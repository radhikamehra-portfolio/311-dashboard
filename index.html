Kick it in the Park — 311 Request Analysis Dashboard

This dashboard analyzes 311 requests for the Kick it in the Park 2026 event series. The data covers the event day, one day before, and one day after each event to measure the collective impact on service requests. The timeframe spans June 10 – July 20, 2026 (40 days), limited to a 0.25-mile radius around each event location. Data was joined and cleaned using Python.

Methodology

These visualizations match the locator_gis_returned_address column with Google Maps Geocoding API metadata (google_raw_tags), categorizing each location as Residential Building, Park/Public Facility, Other/Street, or Unclassified.


Since the complainant's home address is unavailable, this is the closest proxy for understanding who is filing the request — a resident nearby, or an event attendee at the park.


Key insight: Graffiti Removal is overwhelmingly tied to Park / Public Facility (1,283 of 1,636), while Illegal Dumping Item Pickup is predominantly Residential Building (171 of 371). Homeless Encampment clusters under Other / Street Property (99) and Unclassified (86).

2025 311 Request Analysis — Same Locations, Same Period

In order to get a better understanding of the impact of the Kick it in the Park 2026 event series, we are analyzing 311 service requests for the exact same 20 park locations during the exact same calendar period (June 10 – July 20) one year prior, in 2025 — when no Kick it in the Park events took place. Why only 212 requests? The raw 2025 data contains 6,288 records, but the 2026 data was filtered using a precise per-park bounding box (±0.25 mi lat/lon from center). Applying that same bounding box to 2025 yields 212 records — all Graffiti Removal by city field crews. The remaining ~6,076 are real requests that simply occurred outside the event radius.

Inferred Building Type by Park — 2025 (Bbox)

Methodology & 2025 Context

As with the 2026 analysis, building type was inferred by matching the locator_gis_returned_address to Google Maps Geocoding API metadata (google_raw_tags). Raw tags were categorized into Residential Building, Other/Street Property, or Commercial Building.


Note that the 2025 dataset has only 3 building types vs 2026's 5 — there are no Park / Public Facility or Unclassified entries. This is consistent with the data being entirely Driver Self Report: city crews log graffiti at street addresses, not at park facility coordinates, so the Google tags return residential or street-type results.


Key contrast with 2026: In 2026, Park / Public Facility was the dominant building type (367 records — driven by MacArthur Park event attendees). In 2025, that category is completely absent, confirming that near-park activity in 2025 was crew-driven street-level work, not event-driven park usage.


The full 6,288 raw 2025 dataset shows the same pattern at scale: 3,626 Residential (57.7%), 2,659 Other/Street (42.3%), 3 Commercial — no park facility type at all.

% Change in Requests: 2024 → 2026 (the meaningful baseline comparison)

Comparing 2024 (resident baseline) to 2026 (event year): MacArthur Park +121%, Whitsett Fields +23%, Stoner RC −27%. Parks that increased in 2026 relative to 2024 signal genuine event-driven uplift; those that decreased likely reflect the shift from the legacy 311 system (which captured Bulky Items and crew logs) to the new Salesforce platform (which skews toward resident self-service).

Top Complaint Types — 2024 vs 2025 vs 2026

Entirely new 2026 categories — Dockless Mobility Enforcement, Item Pickups, Information-Only — did not exist in prior years, confirming that the event series and the new platform together reshaped the complaint landscape.

Highest Complaint Category — Overall (2024)

In 2024, Bulky Items (1,376 — 36.8%) is the top complaint, followed by Graffiti Removal (1,059 — 28.3%) and Homeless Encampment (615 — 16.4%). This is a typical summer resident pattern: bulk waste, street-level graffiti, and encampment clearance dominate. Highest Complaint Category by Each Park (2024)

MacArthur Park stands out with Homeless Encampment as top complaint (457), unlike all other parks where Bulky Items or Graffiti leads. Hansen Dam Pool shows Dead Animal Removal as its top complaint (2 of 4 total), reflecting its less urbanised setting.

Hourly Complaint Count — 2024 (All Days)

The 2024 hourly curve shows a broad afternoon peak (3–5pm) with steady morning activity from 9am. There is no sharp noon spike seen in 2026's Event Day pattern — instead, demand is spread across the working day. The late-evening 8–9pm spike seen in 2026 is absent here.

Requests by Day of Week (2024)

Tuesday sees the highest requests (799), with a midweek peak typical of resident service reporting. Weekend activity drops sharply, unlike 2026 where events fell on both weekdays and weekends.

Mode of Contact (2024)

In 2024, 45.1% used the Mobile App, 29.0% called, and 16.7% used Self Service (web). Only 8.8% are Driver Self Report — crews logging graffiti. This is a resident-driven dataset with diverse reporting channels, in sharp contrast to 2025's 99.5% Driver Self Report.
